Why 1900 kcal is often too low for an active athlete

5 min read · HybridFuelCo

Most active athletes chronically undereat, hoping to lose fat faster. Usually the opposite happens: the weight stalls, energy drops, and training feels harder than it should.

Your maintenance is higher than you think

"Maintenance" is what your body burns in a day — training included. For someone who runs and lifts, that's often between 2400 and 2800 kcal, not 1900. That 1900 figure comes from twenty-year-old diet logic, not from what your body actually needs now.

You can estimate it with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula plus an activity factor — which is exactly what our app does in one tap: your personal maintenance and macros, free.

Undereating isn't a faster route to your goal. It's the fastest route to a plateau.

Why cutting too deep backfires

An extreme deficit costs you muscle, not just fat — and when you train hard, holding onto muscle is the whole game. On top of that: you recover worse, sleep lighter, hunger wins, and within a few weeks you quit. Then the weight comes back. That's not a lack of willpower; it's biology reacting to too steep a cut.

Do it this way instead

  • Find your maintenance first — calculate it or let the app do it.
  • Pick a moderate deficit: roughly 300–500 kcal below maintenance, good for about 0.5–1% of bodyweight per week. Sustainable, and it protects muscle.
  • Keep protein high — around 2 grams per kilo of bodyweight per day.
  • Don't fear carbs around training. That's fuel, not the enemy.

Eat enough to fuel the work, cut moderately, and give it time. A slow, boring approach beats any crash week.
